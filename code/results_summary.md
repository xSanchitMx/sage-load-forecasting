# Results summary

Real-time self-adaptive agentic load forecasting: UCI ElectricityLoadDiagrams, 8 clients, hourly, prequential (test-then-train) evaluation on the last 35% of each series (9,207 hours per client). All numbers are means over the 8 clients on the test block; every setting was chosen on the validation block first. Generated by `make_results_summary.py`.

**Final system, SAGE (Self-Adaptive aGEntic forecaster):** a pool of 5 forecasters (seasonal-naive, Holt-Winters, SARIMA, LightGBM, online linear), combined by MELO (Wang, Goude & Yao 2026). On top sits our agentic layer: ADWIN + Page-Hinkley drift detection, follow-up refits at +24/+72/+168 h after an alarm, and a contextual serving agent that re-weights MELO and the models each hour.

## 2. Injected demand shifts (MAPE % / CV-RMSE %)

Two events per client per scenario: level (+30%, later back), ramp (+30% over 4 weeks, later back), amplitude (daily swing x1.6, later back), temporary (+40% for one week, twice). Daily refit = refitting the models every 24 h.

| Method | none | level | ramp | amplitude | temporary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MELO (weekly refit) | 4.82 / 6.47 | 4.90 / 6.61 | 4.86 / 6.50 | 8.70 / 7.68 | 4.94 / 6.80 |
| **SAGE** | 4.70 / 6.34 | 4.75 / 6.45 | 4.74 / 6.40 | 8.38 / 7.59 | 4.79 / 6.58 |
| MELO, daily refit (21 refits/wk) | - | 4.84 / 6.48 | 4.80 / 6.39 | 8.95 / 7.59 | 4.88 / 6.57 |

Shift detection (SAGE): level 10/16 events caught, median delay 25 h; ramp 5/16 events caught, median delay 157 h; amplitude 9/16 events caught, median delay 81 h; temporary 14/16 events caught, median delay 30 h. False alarms about 0.20 per week.

The amplitude MAPE is inflated by one client (MT_135), whose near-zero hours become almost zero under the stretch. CV-RMSE is the fairer measure there.

## 3. Statistical significance

Diebold-Mariano test on hourly losses (Newey-West, lag 24 h) for each of 40 series (8 clients x 5 scenarios), plus a Wilcoxon signed-rank test across the series.

| Comparison | loss | better in | sig. better | sig. worse | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SAGE vs MELO alone | absolute | 21/40 | 17 | 5 | 0.0134 |
| SAGE vs MELO alone | squared | 22/40 | 3 | 1 | 0.0372 |
| SAGE vs MELO alone | abs. % error | 20/40 | 17 | 8 | 0.0248 |

## 4. Main findings

1. **SAGE vs MELO (2026):** lowest MAPE and CV-RMSE of every method on the real data (4.70 / 6.34 vs 4.82 / 6.47), lower MAPE and CV-RMSE in every shift scenario, and significantly better overall (Wilcoxon p 0.01-0.04), though significantly worse in 5-8 of 40 series. Its MAE is slightly higher: the agent trades a little absolute accuracy for better percentage accuracy in low-load hours.
2. **Where it helps most:** MT_105 (real 5x load jump on 23 Oct 2014, CV-RMSE 5.79 vs 6.93; 84% of that squared-error gain is one hour where MELO forecast 1,993 kW vs 824 actual) and MT_135 (inverted night-peak profile, MAPE 11.53 vs 12.48; MELO over-forecasts its low-load hours). On the four most regular clients SAGE and MELO are within 0.1 MAPE point. It matches MELO refitted daily on CV-RMSE and beats it on MAPE, with about a fifth of the refits.
3. **Ablation:** without the contextual agent the drift response alone gives the same CV-RMSE gain and ~0.1 point higher MAPE; the agent's own contribution is not significant (p 0.25-0.33).
4. **What did not work:**
    - refitting on post-shift data only (one week of data is too little for SARIMA / LightGBM)
    - resetting MELO after alarms
    - learning the agent from squared errors
    - the initial top-3 inverse-error blend, which every adaptive combiner beat
5. **Bugs found and fixed along the way:**
    - a Holt-Winters seasonal-phase error
    - SARIMA forecasting up to 168 h blind
    - a drift detector that behaved as a weekly timer
    - a recovery metric that always returned 1 h
